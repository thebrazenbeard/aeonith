# Blind reader immersion card — human study worksheet

**Status:** Small optional paper/workflow instrument, **not** a validated psychometric scale or automatic Aeonith pass/fail test. This form can be used with any original, permissioned first-person fiction excerpt. Complete the **unprimed** portion before revealing the author's intent or any narrator-name constraint.

## Private session header (retain outside public repo)

- Work / version / section (local reference only):
- Reader identifier (pseudonymous):
- Consent / permissions / disclosure limitations:
- Excerpt word count and selection method:
- Date, reading medium and whether the reader saw synopsis or prior draft:

Do not place completed forms, passages, author-identifying notes, consent material or fingerprints in this public repository.

## Phase 1 — unprimed response

Do not tell the reader that the narrator is deliberately unnamed. Ask without leading:

1. What happened in this passage? Describe it in your own words.
2. Who did you feel closest to? Where did that closeness increase or break?
3. At what exact sentence, if any, did you become bored, confused, or disbelieving?
4. Which decision felt emotionally credible, even if you disagreed with it? Which did not?
5. What did you infer about the narrator from **observable wording and action**, and what remains unknown?
6. Did any unusual narrative device attract your attention? If yes, what and why?
7. What did you expect to happen next? Would you willingly read another scene? Why?

Record concrete passage pointers and **verbatim reader feedback only in private custody**. A positive response is human opinion, not general-population evidence.

## Phase 2 — post-reveal diagnosis

Only after Phase 1 is locked:

- Explain that the author intended a first-person narrator whose given name is not supplied.
- Had the reader noticed independently? If yes, identify the sentence where they noticed; if no, ask whether the reveal alters the experience.
- Did any line seem designed to conceal a name? Record exact location.
- Would a stronger name-avoidance tactic make the story feel more contrived?
- Was the emotional identification genuine, or did vagueness merely hide contradictions?

## Reviewer conclusion

Write four separate findings, each with source bounds:

- **VOICE:** concrete strengths / falsifiers / unknowns
- **IMMERSION:** unprimed evidence / contrary evidence / unknowns
- **STRUCTURE:** event causality / motivation / missing context
- **NAME DEVICE:** unnoticed / noticed / indeterminate; never infer from automation alone

The reviewer may recommend **REVISE**, **CONTINUE TO NEXT EXCERPT**, or **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE**. Do not publish a quantitative "reader-identification score" from this form. Do not treat one friendly reading, an LLM simulation of a reader, or an Aeonith format pass as an independent human study.
